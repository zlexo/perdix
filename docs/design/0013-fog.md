# RFC 0013: Fog, sky and an MSAA switch

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Draft |
| **Milestone** | M9 Horizon (RFC 0011's item 11; the distance fog M7 had, pulled forward on 2026-10-09) |
| **Issue** | #426 |

## Summary

Perdix gets Minecraft Java Edition 26.2's daytime overworld fog: a haze that thickens with distance and a band that closes the view at its edge, the thicker of the two at each point, mixed toward 26.2's fog color on terrain and bodies. The sky fades from its color overhead to the fog color at the horizon as 26.2's does, so fogged land meets the sky with no seam. Chunks that stream in fade from the fog color over 0.75 seconds, as 26.2's sections do. Every distance, color, curve and duration is the game's, read from its 26.2 jar, up to 26.2's farthest view of 512 blocks. Past it, as RFC 0011 decided, the haze and the band grow with the view so that its edge looks like 26.2's at 32 chunks, whether the view ends at 1 km or 16 km. The fog also hides where RFC 0011's coarse levels begin and softens the far hills' shimmer. For the edges that still shimmer, the app gains an MSAA switch, off by default as 26.2 has none.

## Motivation

- **The world ends at a hard edge.** At 512 chunks (`--horizon`) the terrain stops in a cliff against a flat `#78A7FF` sky, and at every view distance chunks pop in at the edge as they stream. 26.2 hides both with fog: its terrain is solid fog color at the view's edge.
- **Far hills shimmer.** In the 2026-10-09 playtest (2940x1846, before RFC 0011) distant hills showed moire: blocks smaller than a pixel. The coarse levels removed most of it, and flat colors past level 2 the rest of the texture moire. Geometry edges still alias, which the fog only softens, hence the switch.
- **The look.** Haze is part of how Minecraft looks: the same land without it reads as another game. Perdix's rule is that what the player sees is 26.2's.
- **RFC 0011 needs it.** Its item 11 (#400) builds the far fog; its open question 3 settled the principle: 26.2's fog with distances scaled to the view. Its swaps' fade from the fog color (#396) was left for this RFC, since there was no fog color yet.

## Goals and non-goals

Goals, each checked by a test:

1. 26.2's fog ramps, color and blend on the chunks (opaque and cutout) and the bodies, exactly for views up to 512 blocks: the fog value at a pixel within 1/255 of 26.2's formula.
2. Past 512 blocks of view, the edge looks like 26.2's at its farthest: 25% haze at half the view, 45% at nine tenths, solid at the edge, at every view distance.
3. 26.2's daytime sky gradient, the fog fraction at each elevation within 2/255 of the disc model below; no seam above 3/255 where fogged land meets the sky.
4. A chunk shown where nothing was drawn fades from the fog color over 0.75 s, linearly, as 26.2's Chunk Fade does; a setting from 0 to 2 s.
5. An MSAA switch in the overlay: off, 2x, 4x, resolved into the frame; off by default.
6. Fog, sky and fade together under 0.3 ms of GPU time at 2940x1846 on the M3.

Non-goals:

- **Time of day and weather.** The colors here are 26.2's noon overworld; sunrise tints, the sun, moon and stars, clouds, rain (which moves both ramps) and biome colors belong to a later sky RFC with M6 Light and M7 Water & Sky. This RFC keeps the colors in one place for it.
- **Fluid and effect fogs:** underwater, lava, powder snow, blindness, darkness. With fluids (M7) and effects.
- **26.4's improved fog**, which fades terrain into a sampled image of the sky: not in 26.2.
- **Other anti-aliasing:** FXAA and temporal anti-aliasing.
- **Fog on the debug cubes, name tags and overlay.** Name tags show at most 64 blocks away, where the haze is 6%.

## Prior art

**Minecraft Java Edition 26.2**, read from the jar (values and behavior only, as RFC 0009 allows; the classes named are where each lives):

History, from the research for RFC 0011: 1.18.1 brought the cylindrical band; 1.21.6 split the haze from it and set the 1,024 blocks; 1.21.11 made the distances data and added the chunk fade (25w44a).

**Far-view renderers.** Distant Horizons turns vanilla fog off and draws its own from 0.4 to 1.0 of its radius, exponential squared, which closes the view earlier and hides more. Voxy keeps vanilla's haze but caps it near vanilla's own distance and fades its far terrain into the sky over the last tenth. Bedrock's fog runs from 92% of the render distance. Cubyz uses a height fog that keeps mountain tops clear.

**MSAA on tile-based GPUs.** Apple's GPUs keep a frame's multisampled pixels in tile memory and resolve them as each tile is written out, so the multisampled images need no memory of their own (memoryless, or Vulkan's lazily allocated transient attachments); the cost is shading the edge samples and the resolve.

## Design

### Fog distances

D is the view distance in blocks: 32 per chunk, up to 16,384 with `--horizon`. Two ramps, each linear and clamped to 0 and 1:

- **The haze**, on the distance from the eye: from 0 to **H = max(1,024, 2D)**.
- **The band**, on max(distance across, height difference): from D - w to D, with **w = max(4, D / 10)**.

The fog value is the larger. For any D up to 512 blocks this is 26.2's formula: its haze ends at 1,024, which is 2D at its farthest view, and its band's clamp(D / 10, 4, 64) is max(4, D / 10) up to 640 blocks. Past 512 blocks the two grow with D, so the edge keeps the proportions of 26.2's at 32 chunks. Open question 1 compares this with RFC 0011's wording, the haze ending at D.

| View | H | Band | Fog at half the view | at 0.9 D | at D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 chunks, 256 blocks | 1,024 | 230 to 256 | 13% | 23% | 100% |
| 16 chunks, 512 blocks | 1,024 | 461 to 512 | 25% | 45% | 100% |
| 32 chunks, 1,024 blocks | 2,048 | 922 to 1,024 | 25% | 45% | 100% |
| 512 chunks, 16,384 blocks | 32,768 | 14,746 to 16,384 | 25% | 45% | 100% |

The view distance is the one the world holds: when RFC 0006's memory budget lowers it, the fog closes in with it, as 26.2's follows its option.

### Fog color and blend

The fog color is `#C0D8FF` and the sky's `#78A7FF`, 26.2's at noon. Under 512 blocks of view the fog color moves toward the sky's by 26.2's factor, with D / 16 as its chunks. Both colors live in one `FogLook`, which the later sky RFC will drive.

26.2 blends in gamma-encoded values. Perdix shades in linear light into sRGB images, so a linear mix would come out lighter: half fog over a mid-gray `#606060` gives `#909CB0` in 26.2 and `#9AAAC5` mixed linearly. The three blends here (fog, sky, fade) therefore encode both colors, mix, and decode, which matches 26.2's on screen; everything else stays linear.

The distances are computed per vertex from the camera, as the vertex shaders already place each vertex relative to the camera's block, and interpolated; the ramps and the mix run per fragment. Coarse chunks' vertices are already in blocks, so the fog needs nothing per level.

### The sky

The frame clears to the fog color, as 26.2's does. Before the chunks, one full-screen triangle at the far plane draws the sky: for each pixel, the elevation of its view ray. Where the ray meets 26.2's disc, 16 blocks above the eye and 512 blocks across from its center, its distance there is interpolated as across the disc, from 16 blocks at the center to 512.25 at the rim, and the sky color is fogged by it from 0 to 512 blocks. Below the rim's elevation, 1.79 degrees, the pixel stays fog-colored. The sky's distances are 26.2's own and do not grow with the view: the sky looks the same at any distance.

| Elevation | 90° | 30° | 10° | 5° | 3° | 2° | under 1.79° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fog in the sky | 3% | 8% | 20% | 38% | 61% | 90% | 100% |

Land at the view's edge is pure fog color, and so is the sky just above the horizon, so the two meet without a seam.

### Chunk fade-in

An arena record keeps the frame time it was first shown. A chunk shown where nothing was drawn the frame before (streaming in, after a teleport) fades from the fog color to its own over the fade time, linearly, before the fog: 26.2's rule for a section's first mesh. A remesh keeps the time, as in 26.2. A chunk shown by a level swap, whose parent or children the same swap hides, shows at once: its column was drawn the frame before, at another level, and a fog-colored flash there would be new. That differs from RFC 0011's design, which faded newly shown coarse chunks (open question 3). Each drawn chunk's fade goes to the shader with its origin, as RFC 0011 put its level there.

### The MSAA switch

The overlay's Graphics section gets an anti-aliasing choice: off, 2x, 4x, among the counts the device offers for both color and depth. With it on, the world's pass draws into multisampled color and depth images (transient, lazily allocated where the device offers it, as Apple's tile memory does) and resolves color into the frame's image by averaging; the overlay then draws over the resolved image, single-sampled. The world's pipelines are made per sample count. A change rebuilds the attachments and pipelines between two frames; it stalls that one frame. The headless capture reads the resolved image.

### Settings and overlay

- **Fog**, on by default: off for looking at the terrain's own colors, as 26.2's debug toggle does.
- **Chunk fade**: 0 to 2 s, default 0.75 s, 26.2's Chunk Fade option.
- **Anti-aliasing**: off, 2x, 4x; a styled list, as the overlay's other choices.

### API sketch

```cpp
namespace vx::render {

// RFC 0013's fog ramps for a view, in blocks: 26.2's formula up to 512
// blocks, scaled past it.
struct FogRamps {
    float haze_end;   // the haze: from 0 to this, on the distance
    float band_start; // the band: on max(across, height difference)
    float band_end;
};
[[nodiscard]] FogRamps fog_ramps(double view_blocks) noexcept;

// 26.2's noon overworld, in one place for the later sky RFC.
struct FogLook {
    Color fog; // #C0D8FF
    Color sky; // #78A7FF
};
// The fog color for a view: under 512 blocks, 26.2's pull toward the sky.
[[nodiscard]] Color fog_color(const FogLook& look, double view_blocks) noexcept;

enum class Antialiasing : std::uint8_t { off, x2, x4 };

struct FrameInputs {
    // ... as now, with `sky` replaced by:
    FogLook look;
    double view_blocks = 1'024.0;  // the view distance the world holds
    bool fog = true;
    float chunk_fade_seconds = 0.75F;
};

class Renderer {
public:
    // Rebuilds the world's attachments and pipelines before the next frame.
    [[nodiscard]] Result<void> set_antialiasing(Antialiasing samples);
};

} // namespace vx::render
```

The shaders reach a per-frame block through one device address in each pass's push constants: the fog color, the ramps, the sky color and the frame's time for the fade. The chunks' push constants have 16 of their 128 bytes free, enough for it.

## Alternatives considered

- **The haze ending at the view distance**, RFC 0011's sentence (open question 1): twice as thick as 26.2 ever is. Half the view at 50% fog and the last tenth over 90%; at 16 km, everything past 13 km is mostly fog, which spends most of the view RFC 0011 built.
- **Distant Horizons' far fog** from 0.4 of the view: hides more, and is not 26.2's look.
- **A height fog** (Cubyz): keeps mountain tops clear, also not 26.2's.
- **Fading terrain into the sky image** (26.4, Voxy): needs the sky drawn first and terrain alpha; not in 26.2.
- **The sky as geometry**, 26.2's disc of triangles: the full-screen pass gives the same colors with no mesh and no seams between its triangles.
- **Fading level swaps too** (RFC 0011's design, open question 3): fog-colored flashes where levels change. The first change is 660 blocks out at the default quality, under 2% haze at 512 chunks of view (32% at 32 chunks).
- **A cross-fade between levels**: both levels drawn and dithered for a moment; worth it only if the swaps' pop shows through the fog.
- **FXAA or temporal anti-aliasing:** FXAA smears texture detail, and temporal anti-aliasing blurs and ghosts in motion. MSAA touches only edges and is cheap on tile-based GPUs.
- **Linear blends:** simpler, but visibly lighter than 26.2's at middle fog values.

## Performance budget

On the M3 at 2940x1846, by the overlay's GPU time on the default flight, once per pull request (no sweep):

- **Fog:** two lengths per vertex, two ramps, an encode, a mix and a decode per fragment. Under 0.2 ms.
- **Sky:** one triangle. Apple's hidden-surface removal shades only the pixels no terrain covers. Under 0.1 ms.
- **Fade:** one float per drawn chunk, and a mix only while a chunk fades.
- **MSAA 4x:** reported, not gated; shown in the overlay beside the frame time.

## Test plan

- **Ramps (unit):** `fog_ramps` equals 26.2's formula for every whole view from 4 to 512 blocks, is continuous at 512, and past it ends the haze at 2D and starts the band at 0.9 D; the fog value at D is 1. `fog_color` gives 26.2's pull at 2, 12 and 24 of its chunks and none from 512 blocks.
- **Fog on screen (headless GPU):** quads at known distances in a known color; each pixel's fog value within 1/255 of the CPU's, and the half-fog pixel equal to the encoded mix within 1/255, which the linear mix misses by 10/255.
- **Sky:** captures at known pitches; the center row equals the disc model's color at its elevation within 2/255, and below the rim it is the fog color.
- **Seam:** RFC 0011's horizon camera at 512 chunks; the colors just above and below the land's top edge differ by under 3/255.
- **Fade:** with a fake clock, a chunk streamed in is fog-colored in its first frame and its own color 0.75 s later; a chunk shown by a level swap is its own color in its first frame; a remeshed chunk does not fade again.
- **MSAA:** a capture of a block's edge has blended pixels at 4x and none when off; switching 20 times leaks nothing (VMA's statistics) and the validation layers stay silent.
- **Planted bugs**, each shown red: a linear blend, the haze fixed at 1,024, the band capped at 64 blocks, the band on the spherical distance, the sky's distances scaled with the view, a fade on a level swap, MSAA without resolve.
- **By eye**, the Test List: flying at 512 chunks, the hills fade into a pale haze and the edge cannot be seen; the horizon meets the sky with no line; after a teleport, chunks fade in; 4x smooths block edges.

## Open questions

1. **The haze at long views** (taste). Recommendation: **26.2's formula up to its 512-block view, and past it the haze to 2D and the band from 0.9 D**, which keeps 26.2's edge at any distance: 25% fog at half the view, 45% at nine tenths, then the band. The other way, RFC 0011's sentence: the haze ends at D, so 50% at half the view and 90% at nine tenths; at 16 km most of the far view is fog.
2. **The sky** (look). Recommendation: **26.2's daytime gradient** as above, fixed distances. The other way: the flat sky of today, with a visible line where fog-colored land meets `#78A7FF`.
3. **Fading level swaps** (look; amends RFC 0011). Recommendation: **fade only chunks that appear where nothing was drawn**, as 26.2 fades only new sections, and swap levels at once. The other way: RFC 0011's fade of newly shown coarse chunks: fog-colored flashes where levels change, 660 blocks out under 2% haze at 512 chunks of view.
4. **MSAA by default** (look and cost). Recommendation: **off**, as 26.2 has no MSAA; the switch is there to compare. The other way: 4x on.
5. **The blend** (feel). Recommendation: **encoded, as 26.2 blends**. The other way: linear, lighter at middle fog values.
